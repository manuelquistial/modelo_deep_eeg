ARTICLE                  INFO                                ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                    Motor Imagery (MI)-based Brain–Computer Interface (BCI) technology has demonstrated growing potential
Motor Imagery (MI)                                           in neurorehabilitation. However, numerous significant couplings in scalp electroencephalography (EEG)
Decoding                                                     signals, primarily induced by zero-lag volume conduction and common source effects, hinder the decoding
Partition
                                                             performance. To address these challenges, this study proposes a novel decoding model, termed NCD-
Imaginary coherence (iCOH )
                                                             PMSTCNN: the Partitioned Multi-scale SpatioTemporal Convolutional Neural Network (PMSTCNN) based on
Neural Connectivity Decoupler (NCD)
                                                             a Neural Connectivity Decoupler (NCD). This model employs imaginary coherence (𝑖𝐶𝑂𝐻) to measure the
                                                             functional connectivity between electrodes, effectively suppressing the volume conduction effects. The graph
                                                             Laplacian random walk operator normalizes EEG signals, smooths adjacency graphs, and further mitigates
                                                             volume conduction to achieve neural connectivity disentanglement. A task-driven partitioning strategy divides
                                                             electrodes into four functional partitions, while hemispheric symmetry guides the bipartite partitioning. The
                                                             PMSTCNN extracts features and the final decoding is performed by a multilayer perceptron (MLP). Our model
                                                             achieved state-of-the-art performance on the BCI Competition IV Dataset 2a public dataset, with an average
                                                             accuracy of 86.24% and a kappa value of 81.68. Using this model, we developed an online, brain-controlled
                                                             wheelchair system. Furthermore, this study captures more realistic neurophysiological interactions, offers a
                                                             new perspective on EEG signal decoding, and shows significant practical value in neuroscience and motor
                                                             rehabilitation applications.
